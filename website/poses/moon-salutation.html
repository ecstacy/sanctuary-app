Moon Salutation

Chandra Namaskar·चन्द्र नमस्कार·candra namaskāra

Also called: Lunar Salutation

Chandra Namaskar is the evening counterpart to Surya Namaskar. Same vinyasa-style flow, but slower and cooling. Useful for pitta-heavy users who feel overstimulated by morning sun salutations, and for evening practice when heat-building isn't the goal.

Benefits

  • Cooling and grounding — the energetic counterpart to the heating Sun Salutation
  • Lateral mobility focus — different from the Sun's flexion/extension dominance
  • Calming flow useful for evening practice or stress-heavy days
  • Honors the moon's energy in the traditional pairing with Sun Salutations

How to practise Moon Salutation

  1. Stand at the front of the mat with hands at heart, feet together (Tadasana).
  2. INHALE: sweep the arms out and overhead, palms touching.
  3. EXHALE: side bend to the right.
  4. INHALE: return to center.
  5. EXHALE: side bend to the left. INHALE return to center.
  6. EXHALE: step the left foot back into a wide low lunge (Crescent or Anjaneyasana).
  7. INHALE: open into Goddess Pose with both knees wide. EXHALE side bend right, INHALE center, EXHALE side bend left, INHALE center.
  8. Step the right foot back to mirror the lunge on the other side. Reverse the flow back to standing. Repeat 3-5 rounds.

Suggested hold: 300s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women in late pregnancy should reduce the depth of side bends and lunges
  • Don't practice with severe knee, hip, or back injury
  • Skip with severe hamstring or groin strain
  • Avoid with vertigo from the side-bend phases

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Use a wall behind you for stability if balance is unstable in the lunge phases
  • Reduce the depth of the side bends — micro-bend is fine
  • Practice Goddess Pose with the back against a wall
  • Skip the lunges entirely and practice the Tadasana side-bends as a standalone cooling sequence

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha sequence inspired by Sun Salutation but cooling rather than heating. Multiple variants exist; we ship a common Iyengar-influenced form.
