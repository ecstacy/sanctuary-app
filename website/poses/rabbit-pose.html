Rabbit Pose

Sasangasana·शशासन·śaśāṅgāsana

Also called: Sasankasana, Rabbit

Rabbit is the gentle inversion most users have never tried — quietly potent for upper-back release. The supported wrist-grip means most of the weight stays in the legs and hands; the head barely bears any load. Useful as a counter-pose to backbends in a sequence.

Benefits

  • Stretches the entire spine in deep flexion — opposite of every backbend
  • Releases the tension between the shoulder blades — desk-work magic
  • Mild inversion calms the nervous system
  • Easier alternative to Sirsasana for users wanting a head-down sensation safely

How to practise Rabbit Pose

  1. Kneel on the floor and sit on your heels (Vajrasana).
  2. Reach back and grasp the heels of your feet with both hands.
  3. Tuck the chin and place the crown of the head on the mat in front of the knees.
  4. Inhale and lift the hips up, rolling the body forward onto the crown.
  5. Most of the weight stays in the legs and hands — only 10-20% on the crown.
  6. The forehead approaches the knees as the spine rounds in deep flexion.
  7. Hold, breathing steadily. To exit, lower the hips back to the heels and rest.

Suggested hold: 30s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't practice with neck injury or cervical-spine issues
  • Skip with high blood pressure or glaucoma
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice
  • Avoid with knee injury that prevents kneeling
  • Don't practice on a full stomach

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Place a folded blanket under the knees for cushioning
  • Place a folded blanket under the head for less compression on the crown
  • Skip the heel grip and place the hands on the mat in front of the knees instead
  • Reduce the depth — keep the forehead a few inches off the knees

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha. Bikram-lineage pose; appears in some classical hatha enumerations. Not in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
