Peacock Pose

Mayurasana·मयूरासन·mayūrāsana

Also called: Peacock

Peacock is famously hard to access — most practitioners need months of wrist conditioning before attempting. HYP's enthusiasm for its digestive effects is borne out by abdominal-pressure mechanics. Save for users who already have strong Chaturanga and Bakasana.

Benefits

  • Builds extraordinary wrist, forearm, and core strength
  • HYP claims significant digestive benefits — the abdominal pressure stimulates the gut
  • Mental focus — the balance demands single-pointed attention
  • One of the most distinctive shapes in the asana tradition

How to practise Peacock Pose

  1. Kneel on the mat, knees wide.
  2. Place the palms on the floor between the knees, fingers pointing BACK toward the body.
  3. Lower onto the forearms, pressing the elbows together into the lower abdomen (near the navel).
  4. Stretch the legs out behind you, balls of the feet on the floor.
  5. Shift the weight forward over the elbows; lift the feet off the floor.
  6. The body floats parallel to the floor, balanced entirely on the elbows pressed into the belly.
  7. Hold for several breaths. To exit: lower the feet, push back, sit up.

Suggested hold: 15s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • ADVANCED pose — practice with a teacher
  • Don't practice with wrist, elbow, or shoulder injury
  • Skip with hernia, recent abdominal surgery, or severe digestive issues
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice
  • Avoid with high blood pressure, heart disease, or eye conditions
  • Skip immediately after eating — wait 3-4 hours

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Build wrist strength with Chaturanga before attempting
  • Practice the kneeling forearm position with feet still on the floor first
  • Lift one leg at a time before lifting both
  • Hold for 3-5 seconds initially; build duration over months

Traditional source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.32-33 — Named in HYP Ch. 1. The text praises it as a digestive cure — capable of "digesting even kalakuta poison".
