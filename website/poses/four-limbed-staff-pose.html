Four-Limbed Staff Pose

Chaturanga Dandasana·चतुरङ्ग दण्डासन·caturaṅga daṇḍāsana

Also called: Low Plank, Yogi Push-Up

Chaturanga is the most-injured pose in modern yoga because it's done dozens of times per class without proper form. The fix is simple: drop to the knees, OR don't lower below 90 degrees. Half Chaturanga is a legitimate full-time variant; there's no shame in it.

Benefits

  • Builds the upper-body strength needed for arm balances and inversions
  • Engages the core in a way few other poses do
  • Connective tissue throughout the shoulders adapts and strengthens
  • Foundation of vinyasa transitions — once dialed in, the whole flow practice opens up

How to practise Four-Limbed Staff Pose

  1. Begin in plank pose, shoulders over wrists, body in one line.
  2. Shift the weight slightly forward — shoulders move past the wrists.
  3. Exhale and bend the elbows straight back, hugging them close to the ribs.
  4. Lower until the upper arms are parallel to the floor (90-degree bend at the elbow).
  5. The body stays in one straight line — shoulders, hips, and heels aligned.
  6. Hold steady. Hug the elbows in — don't let them flare out.
  7. To exit: inhale to Upward Dog or lower fully to the floor.

Suggested hold: 15s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't practice with wrist, elbow, or shoulder injury
  • Skip with rotator cuff issues — Chaturanga is the #1 cause of shoulder injury in modern yoga
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice past the first trimester
  • Avoid with carpal tunnel syndrome

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Lower to the knees first — Half Chaturanga is a legitimate full-time practice
  • Stay in plank instead and skip the lower-down
  • Lower only to the elbows-at-90 position, NEVER deeper (the shoulders can't safely go below the elbows)
  • Use a yoga block under the chest as a safety floor — lower until the chest touches

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern asana, central to vinyasa flow practice. Not in HYP Ch. 1.
