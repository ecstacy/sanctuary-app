Lion Pose

Simhasana·सिंहासन·siṃhāsana

Also called: Lion, Lion Breath

Simhasana is the asana most people are too self-conscious to try in a public class — and that's exactly why it works. The combination of facial expression, tongue extension, and forceful exhale releases tension you didn't know was there. HYP explicitly names this practice; modern lineages sometimes drop the tongue extension to make it less awkward, but the original is most effective.

Benefits

  • Releases tension in the jaw, throat, and face — the chronic seat of social-stress holding
  • HYP describes it as removing diseases of the throat
  • Activates the platysma (neck muscle) and tongue — rarely-trained areas
  • Surprisingly emotional — many users find it releases inhibition over time

How to practise Lion Pose

  1. Kneel on the mat with the knees together, sitting back on the heels (Vajrasana base). OR sit cross-legged.
  2. Place the palms on the knees, fingers spread wide.
  3. Inhale deeply through the nose.
  4. Open the mouth wide, extend the tongue as far down toward the chin as possible.
  5. Exhale forcefully through the open mouth, making a "HAAAAA" sound from the throat — the lion's roar.
  6. Widen the eyes — gaze up toward the third eye or down at the tip of the nose.
  7. Repeat 5-8 rounds. After the last round, close the eyes and rest.

Suggested hold: 60s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Skip with severe jaw pain or recent dental surgery
  • Avoid with active throat infection or laryngitis
  • Pregnant women can practice without strain
  • If you have tinnitus or hearing sensitivity, do the roar quietly

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Roar quietly if practicing in a shared space
  • Skip the tongue extension if it strains the tongue or jaw
  • Practice cross-legged if the kneeling base is uncomfortable
  • Do fewer rounds initially — 3 rounds builds the comfort to do more

Traditional source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.50-52 — Named in HYP Ch. 1. The text describes the wide-eyed gaze, the tongue extension, and the roaring exhale — explicitly called out as part of the practice.
