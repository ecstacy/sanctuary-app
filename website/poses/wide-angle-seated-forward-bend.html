Wide-Angle Seated Forward Bend

Upavishta Konasana·उपविष्टकोणासन·upaviṣṭa koṇāsana

Also called: Seated Straddle Forward Fold

Wide-Angle Seated Forward Fold is the seated counterpart to Prasarita Padottanasana — but with the support of the floor under the legs. Hold for 1-3 minutes for fascial release through the inner thighs. Use a folded blanket under the seat without exception.

Benefits

  • Stretches the inner thighs (adductors) — distinct from feet-together forward folds
  • Releases the hamstrings of both legs simultaneously
  • Calms the nervous system through forward-fold geometry
  • Often used in yin-yoga sequences as a 3-5 minute hip release

How to practise Wide-Angle Seated Forward Bend

  1. Sit on a folded blanket on the floor.
  2. Extend both legs out wide to the sides — as wide as your hamstrings comfortably allow.
  3. Flex both feet so toes point straight up. Engage the thighs gently.
  4. Place your hands on the floor in front of you. Inhale and lengthen the spine tall.
  5. Exhale and walk your hands forward, hinging from the hips.
  6. Lead with the chest, not the head. Stop where the back wants to round.
  7. Bend the knees if the hamstrings feel pulled.

Suggested hold: 90s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women shouldn't fold deeply — keep a long spine and shallow hinge
  • Don't practice with herniated disc or acute lower-back injury
  • Avoid with hamstring or groin tear
  • Skip with severe sciatica

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Sit on a folded blanket to tilt the pelvis forward — most users need this
  • Bend the knees if the hamstrings are tight
  • Walk the hands forward only as far as the spine stays long
  • Bolster across the legs to rest the chest down (restorative variant)

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha. "Upaviṣṭa" = seated, "kona" = angle. Not in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
