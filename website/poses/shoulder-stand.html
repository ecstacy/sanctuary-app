Shoulder Stand

Sarvangasana·सर्वाङ्गासन·sarvāṅgāsana

Also called: Shoulderstand, Queen of Asanas (Iyengar)

Sarvangasana is a real pose — not a beginner shape. The blanket setup is non-negotiable for protecting the cervical spine. Most users get most of the benefit from Halasana with feet on a chair, or from Legs Up the Wall — recommend those first.

Benefits

  • Powerful nervous-system reset through deep inversion
  • Stimulates the thyroid via throat compression
  • Strengthens the shoulders, upper back, and core
  • Traditionally called "the queen of asanas" in modern lineages — paired with Sirsasana, the king

How to practise Shoulder Stand

  1. Stack 2-3 folded blankets at the top of your mat. Lie down so your shoulders are on the blankets and your head is on the mat below them — this protects the cervical spine.
  2. Bring the arms by your sides, palms down.
  3. Press the arms into the floor and swing the legs up and over the head into Halasana.
  4. Bend the elbows and place the hands on the lower back, fingers pointing toward the spine.
  5. Slowly lift the legs up toward the ceiling, stacking the hips over the shoulders.
  6. The body forms one long line from shoulders to feet. Gaze at the toes.
  7. To exit: lower the legs back into Halasana, then support the lower back with the hands and roll down slowly.

Suggested hold: 60s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't practice with neck injury, herniated disc, or recent neck surgery — and never practice without the shoulder-blanket setup
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice this discrete inversion
  • Skip during menstruation per personal preference
  • Avoid with high blood pressure, glaucoma, or recent retinal detachment
  • Skip with severe asthma, hiatal hernia, or thyroid issues
  • Don't practice on a full stomach — wait 3-4 hours after eating

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Practice Legs Up the Wall instead — same nervous-system effect, no neck demand
  • Practice with a wall behind you — feet rest on the wall as a half-shoulderstand
  • Build up to the full pose with Halasana first; only progress when Halasana feels stable
  • Use a yoga teacher's hands-on guidance for first practice if possible

Traditional source: Modern hatha 3.78 — Modern derivation of the Hatha Yoga Pradipika 3.78 Viparita Karani mudra family. Sarvangasana as a discrete asana was codified in 20th-century lineages (Krishnamacharya, Iyengar).
