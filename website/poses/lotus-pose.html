Lotus Pose

Padmasana·पद्मासन·padmāsana

Also called: Full Lotus

Lotus is a destination pose — not a starting place. the Hatha Yoga Pradipika describes it as the seat that 'destroys all diseases', but reaching it without injury requires patient hip-opening over months or years. If your hips don't allow lotus today, Sukhasana on a cushion is functionally identical for meditation purposes. Don't trade your knees for an Instagram pose.

Benefits

  • Establishes the most stable upright seat — sit bones, knees, and one ankle each anchor a corner of a triangle
  • Lengthens the spine through structural alignment, not muscular effort
  • The classical pranayama and meditation seat described in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika and Patanjali's Yoga Sutras
  • Long holds become possible because the lower body is locked — only the spine and breath remain to manage

How to practise Lotus Pose

  1. WARNING: don't force this pose. If it doesn't come easily, sit in Sukhasana on a cushion instead — your knees will thank you.
  2. Sit with your legs extended straight in front of you on the floor.
  3. Bend the right knee. Use your hands to lift the right foot and place it on top of the left thigh, sole facing up.
  4. Bend the left knee. Lift the left foot and place it on top of the right thigh, sole facing up.
  5. Both knees should rest on the floor. If either knee floats up, this isn't your pose yet — back off.
  6. Rest your hands on your knees, palms up.
  7. Lengthen the spine. The seat should feel stable, not strained.

Suggested hold: 180s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't force this pose. Tight hips will translate the lotus into knee strain — and knees don't recover from that easily
  • Skip with any history of knee surgery, meniscus injury, or ACL/MCL injury
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice the full lotus
  • Avoid with sciatica or active hip injury
  • Don't practice if Sukhasana already strains the knees

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Practice Ardha Padmasana (half lotus) — one foot on the thigh, the other under
  • Practice Sukhasana with the hips elevated on cushions — same benefits, no knee risk
  • Place a folded blanket under the seat to tilt the pelvis forward
  • If the lotus is available but uncomfortable, hold for shorter durations and switch leg positions

Traditional source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.44-49 — Named explicitly in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1. Six verses describe its construction and value as a meditation seat.
