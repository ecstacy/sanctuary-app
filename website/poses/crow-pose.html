Crow Pose

Bakasana·बकासन·bakāsana

Also called: Crow

Crow is THE gateway arm balance. Most users can land it within 2-4 weeks of focused practice if they have the core and wrist strength. The trick is gaze direction — looking forward (not down) is what makes the balance findable. Use a block under the forehead during the learning curve.

Benefits

  • Most accessible arm balance — the entry point to the wider arm-balance family
  • Builds wrist, forearm, and shoulder strength dramatically
  • Demands single-pointed focus — the gaze IS the practice
  • Confidence-builder once landed — many users find their first arm balance transformative

How to practise Crow Pose

  1. Squat in Malasana (deep squat), feet hip-width apart, knees wide.
  2. Place the palms on the floor in front of the feet, shoulder-width apart, fingers spread wide.
  3. Bend the elbows slightly outward (chaturanga-style — elbows track over the wrists, not flared wide).
  4. Press the knees against the back of the upper arms, just above the elbows.
  5. Shift the weight forward over the hands. Lift the heels and curl the toes off the floor.
  6. Gaze forward — NOT down. The gaze direction is the balance.
  7. Hold for 5-15 seconds. To exit: lower the feet, lift the head, stand up.

Suggested hold: 20s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't practice with wrist, elbow, or shoulder injury
  • Skip with carpal tunnel syndrome
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice past the first trimester
  • Avoid with high blood pressure
  • Skip if you can't yet hold a strong plank for 30 seconds

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Practice with a block under the forehead so falling forward is safe
  • Start with one foot at a time — get comfortable lifting one before both
  • Place a folded blanket in front of the hands in case of a forward fall
  • Build wrist and core strength first: plank, side plank, chaturanga

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern arm balance, named for sage Baka. Often confused with Kakasana (Crane); Bakasana has bent arms, Kakasana has straight arms.
