Marichi's Pose A

Marichyasana A·मरीच्यासन A·marīcyāsana

Also called: Sage Marichi's Forward Fold

Marichyasana A is a binding forward fold — uniquely combines hamstring length with shoulder opening. Save it for users who already have steady Paschimottanasana and Janu Sirsasana. The bind isn't the point; the active engagement from trying to bind is what makes the fold deeper than a passive one.

Benefits

  • Combines forward fold + shoulder opener — efficient for desk-tight users
  • The bind teaches active engagement during a forward fold (most folds are passive)
  • Stretches the hamstring of one leg while opening the shoulder of the other
  • Useful in preparation for binding twists later

How to practise Marichi's Pose A

  1. Sit on a folded blanket with both legs extended in front of you.
  2. Bend the right knee and place the right foot flat on the floor, heel close to the right sit bone.
  3. Keep the left leg long and active, foot flexed.
  4. Inhale and lengthen the spine.
  5. Wrap the right arm around the right shin (and around the back of the body) to clasp the left hand behind your back. Or hold a strap between the hands.
  6. Exhale and fold forward over the extended left leg.
  7. Hold for 30-60 seconds. Switch sides.

Suggested hold: 60s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice the bind — try a plain seated forward fold instead
  • Don't practice with shoulder injury or rotator cuff issue — the wrap is intense
  • Skip with herniated disc or severe lower-back injury
  • Avoid with hamstring tear

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Use a strap between the hands if they don't reach behind the back
  • Skip the bind entirely — practice as Janu Sirsasana with the bent knee straight up instead of out
  • Sit on a higher blanket to tilt the pelvis forward
  • Bend the extended knee if hamstrings feel pulled

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern Iyengar/Ashtanga lettering of poses named for the sage Marichi (a son of Brahma). The Hatha Yoga Pradipika does not enumerate the lettered variants.
