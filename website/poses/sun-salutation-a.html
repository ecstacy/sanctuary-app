Sun Salutation A

Surya Namaskar A·सूर्य नमस्कार A·sūrya namaskāra

Also called: Sun Salutation, Surya Namaskara

Surya Namaskar A is the canonical opening sequence of any vinyasa practice — 3-10 rounds builds heat, opens the spine, and trains the breath-led flow that everything else depends on. For users new to yoga, this single sequence is half the practice — most other asanas become accessible once the body knows this flow.

Benefits

  • Most complete single sequence in modern yoga — every major muscle group, every plane of motion
  • Cardiovascular and warming — builds heat for the rest of the practice
  • Trains breath-movement coordination — the foundation of vinyasa
  • Honors the sun's energy in the traditional context

How to practise Sun Salutation A

  1. Stand at the front of the mat with hands in prayer at the heart (Tadasana).
  2. INHALE: sweep the arms out and up overhead, slight backbend (Urdhva Hastasana).
  3. EXHALE: fold forward over the legs, hands to the floor (Uttanasana).
  4. INHALE: lift the chest halfway, fingertips on the shins, long flat back (Ardha Uttanasana).
  5. EXHALE: step or jump back to plank, lower halfway down with elbows close (Chaturanga).
  6. INHALE: roll over the toes and lift the chest into Upward Dog (or low Cobra).
  7. EXHALE: tuck the toes and press back into Downward Dog. Hold for 5 breaths.
  8. INHALE: step or jump forward, halfway lift. EXHALE: fold. INHALE: rise to standing arms overhead. EXHALE: hands to heart.

Suggested hold: 300s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women should skip the prone phases (Chaturanga, Up Dog) — substitute Knees-Chest-Chin and Cobra
  • Don't practice with severe wrist injury — modify or skip Chaturanga
  • Skip with herniated disc, severe lower-back injury, or recent abdominal surgery
  • Avoid with high blood pressure or recent cardiac event — too cardiovascular
  • Don't practice on a full stomach — wait 2-3 hours after eating

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Knees-Chest-Chin instead of Chaturanga for new practitioners — lower the knees, then chest, then chin to the floor
  • Cobra instead of Upward Dog — keeps the hips on the floor
  • Step (don't jump) back and forward
  • Substitute the whole sequence with the Beginner's Sun Salutation: Tadasana → Uttanasana → Plank → Down Dog → Plank → Uttanasana → Tadasana

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha sequence. The flowing-vinyasa form was codified by Krishnamacharya in 20th-century Mysore lineage. Devotional sun-salutation rituals predate the modern asana sequence.
