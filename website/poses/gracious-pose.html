Gracious Pose

Bhadrasana·भद्रासन·bhadrāsana

Also called: Throne Pose, Auspicious Pose, Cobbler Pose (modern interpretation)

Bhadrasana sits in the cluster of HYP-named meditation seats (Padmasana, Siddhasana, Swastikasana, Bhadrasana) — the four primary postures the text endorses. Modern teaching often uses Baddha Konasana interchangeably; we document them separately to preserve the source-text naming for users who want fidelity.

Benefits

  • HYP-named meditation seat — one of the foundational sitting practices
  • Opens the hips through external rotation, accessible over time
  • Stable and grounding — suitable for long holds
  • Connects modern users to one of the original four meditation poses

How to practise Gracious Pose

  1. Sit on a folded blanket with the legs extended.
  2. Bring the soles of the feet together and draw the heels close to the perineum.
  3. Grasp the feet firmly with both hands — fingers wrap around the outer edges.
  4. The knees release toward the floor, supported by cushions if they hover.
  5. Lengthen the spine — crown reaches up, sit bones root down.
  6. Hold gaze either straight ahead or eyes softly closed.
  7. Stay for 3-5 minutes for meditation.

Suggested hold: 180s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't practice with knee or hip injury
  • Avoid with severe sciatica
  • Pregnant women can practice with the feet further from the body (don't compress the abdomen)

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Sit on a folded blanket to elevate the hips
  • Place blocks under each knee for support — never force the knees down
  • Move the feet further from the body for an easier hip stretch
  • If holding the feet strains the lower back, rest the hands on the shins instead

Traditional source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.53-54 — Named in HYP Ch. 1. The text describes it as one of the four primary asanas for meditation. Some modern lineages conflate it with Baddha Konasana; we keep them separate to preserve HYP's distinct naming.
