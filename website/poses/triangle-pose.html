Triangle Pose

Trikonasana·त्रिकोणासन·trikoṇāsana

Also called: Utthita Trikonasana (Extended Triangle)

Trikonasana works the side body — a region modern life almost never reaches. For users with chronic shoulder and neck tension from desk work, the lateral opening here is what hours of foam-rolling can't replicate. Use a block under the bottom hand without shame; almost everyone needs one.

Benefits

  • Lengthens the side body — obliques, intercostals, and quadratus lumborum
  • Opens the hips and groin in lateral flexion (different from forward fold)
  • Strengthens the legs while improving lateral spine mobility
  • Combines almost every alignment principle in one pose — a teaching pose for proprioception

How to practise Triangle Pose

  1. Stand with feet wide apart on the mat, about a leg-length.
  2. Turn the right foot out 90 degrees so the toes point to the right. Slightly turn the left foot in.
  3. Extend both arms out to the sides at shoulder height, palms down.
  4. Reach the right arm forward over the right leg as far as you can, lengthening through the side body.
  5. Lower the right hand down to the shin, ankle, or a yoga block — wherever your spine stays long.
  6. Reach the left arm straight up toward the ceiling. Stack the shoulders.
  7. Look up under the top arm if the neck allows. Switch sides.

Suggested hold: 45s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women in late pregnancy should narrow the stance and avoid the deep side-bend
  • Don't practice with a recent neck injury — keep the gaze straight ahead instead of up
  • Avoid with severe high or low blood pressure
  • Skip with diarrhea or active migraine
  • If you have heart conditions, keep the top arm on the hip rather than overhead

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Place the bottom hand on a block at any height — don't reach for the ankle if it forces the chest down
  • Practice with the back heel against a wall for stability feedback
  • Look straight ahead instead of up if the neck strains
  • Shorten the stance if the front leg feels overworked

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha asana. Codified in 20th-century Iyengar and Krishnamacharya lineages. Not enumerated in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
