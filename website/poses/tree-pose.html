Tree Pose

Vrksasana·वृक्षासन·vṛkṣāsana

Also called: Tree Stand

Balance work is medicine for the scattered mind. Tree pose forces a single-pointed gaze and a slow breath — the same combination meditation cultivates, but easier to access for users who can't sit still yet.

Benefits

  • Trains single-leg stability — translates directly to walking, stairs, and fall prevention
  • Strengthens the small stabilizing muscles of the ankle and arch
  • Concentrative practice — the gaze and the breath become a single anchor
  • A reliable mental-focus reset for racing-mind days

How to practise Tree Pose

  1. Stand with your feet together, weight even on both feet.
  2. Shift all your weight onto your left foot. Press it firmly into the floor.
  3. Bend your right knee and lift your right foot. Place the sole against your inner left thigh or calf — never on the knee.
  4. Bring your hands together in front of your chest, like a prayer.
  5. Find a single still spot at eye level and rest your gaze there.
  6. If steady, slowly extend your arms overhead. Palms can face each other or touch.
  7. Hold for the duration. Lower the arms and foot, then switch sides.

Suggested hold: 45s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women in late pregnancy should practice with the back to a wall for stability
  • Don't practice with severe vertigo or balance disorders without a wall behind you
  • Avoid placing the lifted foot on the knee — always above or below it
  • Skip if you have severe ankle injury in the standing leg

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Place the lifted foot on the ankle (toes still touching the floor) for the easiest variant
  • Keep the toes lightly grounded as a kickstand if balance is unstable
  • Practice with one hand on a wall or chair back
  • Hands at the heart instead of overhead reduces the balance demand

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha balance pose. Not enumerated in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
