Warrior III

Virabhadrasana III·वीरभद्रासन III·vīrabhadrāsana III

Also called: Warrior 3, Airplane Pose

Warrior III is the integration pose of the Virabhadra trilogy — balance, strength, and concentration in one shape. Don't worry if the back leg won't lift to parallel; the practice is the steady horizontal line, not the height.

Benefits

  • Strengthens the back of the standing leg, glutes, and core
  • Trains hip-level proprioception (level pelvis under single-leg load)
  • Single-pointed concentration — great for users with attention drift
  • Builds the postural endurance for longer holds and inversions

How to practise Warrior III

  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart.
  2. Shift all your weight onto your right foot. Slightly bend the right knee for stability.
  3. Hinge forward at your hips, lifting your left leg straight behind you.
  4. Reach your arms straight forward, like an airplane.
  5. Form one long line from fingertips through the back heel.
  6. Keep both hips level — don't let the lifted hip rotate up.
  7. Gaze at a point on the floor about a foot in front of you. Switch sides.

Suggested hold: 30s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice from the second trimester onward
  • Don't practice with severe high blood pressure
  • Avoid with recent knee, hip, or ankle injury in the standing leg
  • Skip if you have severe lower-back pain

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Practice with the fingertips on a wall in front of you for balance support
  • Hands at the heart instead of overhead
  • Lower the back leg toward the floor — height is not the goal
  • Use a chair behind for the lifted leg to rest on

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha. The full balance variant of the Virabhadra trilogy. Not in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
