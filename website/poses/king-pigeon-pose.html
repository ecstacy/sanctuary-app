King Pigeon Pose

Kapotasana·कपोतासन·kapotāsana

Also called: Pigeon (full expression)

Kapotasana is one of the most demanding backbends in modern practice. The combination of hip-flexor flexibility, spinal extension, and shoulder opening required is rare. Save for advanced practitioners with a regular Ustrasana and supported-bridge habit.

Benefits

  • Deepest backbend in the catalog — opens the entire front body
  • Releases the hip flexors and quadriceps simultaneously
  • Major chest and shoulder opening — counters years of forward posture
  • Cardiovascular response — significant adrenaline release; many users feel euphoric afterward

How to practise King Pigeon Pose

  1. PREREQUISITE: established Ustrasana (Camel Pose), Bhujangasana, and Setu Bandha.
  2. Kneel with the knees hip-width apart, thighs vertical.
  3. Inhale, lift the chest, and reach the arms overhead.
  4. Exhale and arch back, leading with the chest. Place the hands on the heels or further forward.
  5. Walk the hands toward the feet, eventually grasping the toes or pulling the head toward the feet.
  6. Drop the head back gently — eyes either looking at the feet or closed.
  7. To exit: walk the hands back, lift the chest, sit up slowly.

Suggested hold: 30s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • ADVANCED pose — work with a teacher before attempting
  • Don't practice with severe lower-back injury, herniated disc, or sacroiliac instability
  • Skip with neck injury or recent shoulder surgery
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice
  • Avoid with high or low blood pressure, vertigo, or migraine

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Build Ustrasana for months before attempting Kapotasana
  • Use a wall — start with the hands on the wall, walk them down gradually over weeks
  • Place blocks against the heels to give the hands a higher target
  • Hold for only 5-10 seconds at first

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern advanced backbend. Not in HYP Ch. 1. Often confused with Eka Pada Rajakapotasana; the full Kapotasana is a kneeling deep backbend, not the asymmetric hip-opener.
