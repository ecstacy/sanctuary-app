Side Angle Pose

Parsvakonasana·पार्श्वकोणासन·pārśvakoṇāsana

Also called: Extended Side Angle, Utthita Parsvakonasana

Side Angle is where users learn what a 'long side body' actually feels like. The geometry forces the underside ribs to elongate — almost no other pose achieves the same lateral stretch with a strong base.

Benefits

  • Lengthens the entire side body from outer foot to fingertips
  • Strengthens the front leg through the long hold
  • Opens the chest in lateral flexion — distinct stretch from forward folds
  • Combines stamina, strength, and lateral mobility in one shape

How to practise Side Angle Pose

  1. Step your feet wide apart on the mat, about a leg-length.
  2. Turn your right foot out 90 degrees. Slightly turn the left foot in.
  3. Bend the right knee toward 90 degrees, knee tracking over the ankle.
  4. Lower your right forearm onto the right thigh, OR rest the right hand on a block outside the right foot.
  5. Reach the left arm overhead, in line with the spine. Palm faces the floor.
  6. Open the chest toward the ceiling, twisting the torso slightly upward.
  7. Look up under the top arm if your neck allows. Switch sides.

Suggested hold: 45s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women in late pregnancy should narrow the stance and use a block
  • Don't practice with severe knee injury in the front leg
  • Avoid with neck injury — keep gaze straight ahead
  • Skip with insomnia (this pose is energizing — not a pre-bed choice)

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Forearm on the front thigh instead of the hand to the floor
  • Block under the bottom hand at any height
  • Top arm on the hip if the shoulder fatigues
  • Shorten the stance if the front knee won't track over the ankle

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha. "Parsva" = side, "kona" = angle. Not in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
