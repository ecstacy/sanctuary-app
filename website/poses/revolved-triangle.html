Revolved Triangle

Parivrtta Trikonasana·परिवृत्त त्रिकोणासन·parivṛtta trikoṇāsana

Also called: Revolved Triangle Pose

Revolved Triangle is the most demanding twist in the catalog at the intermediate level. The combination of hamstring length + balance + rotation reveals exactly how much rotational mobility users actually have. Always use a block under the bottom hand — no exceptions for new practitioners.

Benefits

  • Combines hamstring stretch + deep spinal rotation in one shape
  • Strengthens the standing leg and core
  • Stimulates abdominal organs through the twist
  • More demanding than seated twists — useful for advancing rotational mobility

How to practise Revolved Triangle

  1. Stand with feet wide apart on the mat, but narrower than for Trikonasana — about 3 feet apart.
  2. Turn the right foot out 90 degrees. Square the hips toward the right leg.
  3. Inhale and reach the left arm forward, lengthening through the spine.
  4. Exhale and rotate the torso to the right, lowering the left hand to the floor outside the right foot, OR onto a yoga block.
  5. Reach the right arm straight up toward the ceiling, stacking the shoulders.
  6. Look up under the top arm if the neck allows. Press both feet firmly down.
  7. To exit: inhale and rise back to standing. Switch sides.

Suggested hold: 30s · Breath: Paced

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice — the closed twist compresses the belly
  • Don't practice with herniated disc or severe lower-back injury
  • Skip with high blood pressure or migraine
  • Avoid with neck injury — keep gaze straight ahead
  • Skip with severe sciatica

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Place the bottom hand on a yoga block at any height — not the floor
  • Use a wall behind you for stability — back heel pressing into the wall
  • Reduce the foot-distance to make the balance easier
  • Practice with the top arm on the hip instead of overhead

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha rotational variant of Trikonasana. "Parivṛtta" = revolved. Not in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
