Eagle Pose

Garudasana·गरुडासन·garuḍāsana

Also called: Eagle

Eagle is the most efficient upper-back release in the standing series. The arm wrap targets exactly where keyboard work tightens. Use the modifications without shame — hands-at-heart is a legitimate pose; you still get most of the benefit through the legs.

Benefits

  • Releases the rhomboids and upper trapezius — exactly the area desk work over-tightens
  • Builds single-leg stability with an asymmetric load
  • Concentration practice — the wrap demands a still gaze and slow breath
  • Generates compression that, on release, brings a noticeable wash of warmth

How to practise Eagle Pose

  1. Stand on both feet. Slightly bend both knees.
  2. Shift your weight onto the left foot.
  3. Cross your right thigh over the left, as if sitting cross-legged in a chair.
  4. If accessible, hook the right foot behind the left calf. Otherwise, keep the right toes lightly touching the floor for balance.
  5. Extend your arms straight forward at shoulder height.
  6. Cross your left elbow over the right at right angles. Wrap the forearms and bring the palms to touch (or backs of hands if palms don't reach).
  7. Lift the elbows to shoulder height. Sit slightly lower into the standing leg. Switch sides.

Suggested hold: 30s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't practice with knee, hip, or shoulder injury
  • Pregnant women should skip the leg wrap entirely
  • Avoid with frozen shoulder or rotator cuff injury
  • Skip with severe balance disorders

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Cross the legs without hooking the foot behind the calf (kickstand variant)
  • Hands at the heart in prayer instead of the full arm wrap
  • Hold the opposite shoulders if the arm wrap doesn't reach
  • Practice next to a wall for balance support

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha, named for Garuda, the divine eagle of Vishnu. Not in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
