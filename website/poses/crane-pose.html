Crane Pose

Kakasana·काकासन·kākāsana

Also called: Crane

Crane is the natural progression from Crow once Crow is held effortlessly for 30+ seconds. The straight arms shift the load from the upper arms to the wrists and shoulders, demanding more strength but rewarding with a more sustainable shape.

Benefits

  • Deeper wrist and shoulder strength than Bakasana
  • More elegant arm-balance shape — useful for movement-based flows
  • Preparation for Astavakrasana and other lifted arm balances

How to practise Crane Pose

  1. PREREQUISITE: stable Bakasana (Crow) for 15+ seconds.
  2. Set up exactly as in Bakasana: deep squat, palms on the floor, knees against the upper arms.
  3. Place the knees HIGHER on the arms — closer to the armpits.
  4. Press the floor away and STRAIGHTEN the arms completely.
  5. The hips lift higher, the body more vertical than in Crow.
  6. Hold steady. Gaze forward. The shape is more elegant, less compressed than Crow.
  7. To exit: bend the arms back to Crow, lower the feet, stand up.

Suggested hold: 15s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • ADVANCED — practice with a teacher
  • PREREQUISITE: solid Bakasana before progressing
  • Don't practice with wrist, elbow, or shoulder injury
  • Skip with carpal tunnel syndrome
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Master Bakasana first — Crane is its straight-armed progression
  • Hold for 3-5 seconds initially
  • Practice the lift from a higher base (e.g., a block under each foot) to reduce wrist demand

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern arm balance — the straight-armed sibling of Bakasana (Crow). The deeper version with arms fully extended.
