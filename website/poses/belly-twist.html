Belly Twist

Jathara Parivartanasana·जठर परिवर्तनासन·jaṭhara parivartanāsana

Also called: Reclined Belly Twist, Revolved Abdomen Pose

Belly Twist is one of the most universally-prescribed poses in the catalog — short of injury, almost everyone benefits from it almost every day. Especially useful as the last pose before Savasana to neutralize the spine after asymmetric work.

Benefits

  • Releases the muscles around the lumbar spine and SI joint
  • Stimulates abdominal organs — supports digestion
  • Activates the parasympathetic response — pre-sleep favorite
  • Tri-doshic — works for everyone, which is rare

How to practise Belly Twist

  1. Lie on your back on a flat surface.
  2. Draw both knees in toward your chest.
  3. Extend your arms out to the sides at shoulder height, palms up.
  4. Lower both knees to the right toward the floor, keeping them stacked.
  5. Turn your head to the left.
  6. Both shoulders stay on the floor — don't force the moving shoulder down.
  7. Place a cushion under the moving knees if they don't reach the floor. Switch sides.

Suggested hold: 60s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women should practice an open variant — knees apart, don't compress the belly
  • Don't practice with herniated disc or SI joint instability
  • Avoid after recent abdominal, hip, or back surgery
  • Skip during an active IBS flare

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Place a bolster or cushion under the moving knees so they don't reach the floor
  • Keep the knees stacked rather than letting them slide apart
  • Skip turning the head if the neck is sensitive — gaze straight up
  • Use a single-leg variant: extend the bottom leg long, only the top knee crosses over

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha. "Jaṭhara" = belly, "parivartana" = revolving. Not in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
