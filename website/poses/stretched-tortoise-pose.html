Stretched Tortoise Pose

Uttana Kurmasana·उत्तान कूर्मासन·uttāna kūrmāsana

Also called: Upward Tortoise, Reclining Lotus

Like Kukkutasana, this is a documentary entry — few users will ever practice it. Including it preserves the full HYP Ch. 1 asana list and gives advanced practitioners a recognizable destination.

Benefits

  • Deep withdrawal — the body becomes a closed sphere
  • Activates the bandhas (energetic locks) naturally through the compression
  • Considered preparatory for pranayama in the classical sequence
  • Closes the HYP 1.22-24 tortoise series

How to practise Stretched Tortoise Pose

  1. PREREQUISITE: comfortable Kukkutasana.
  2. From Kukkutasana, slowly roll backward onto the upper back.
  3. Keep the arms threaded through the legs and grasp the ears or back of the head with the hands.
  4. The legs in lotus, now resting on the chest. The body curls inward like a tortoise on its back.
  5. Hold steady. Breathe slowly.
  6. To exit: gently roll back up to Kukkutasana, lower down, release Padmasana.

Suggested hold: 30s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • ADVANCED pose — practice with a teacher
  • PREREQUISITE: solid Kukkutasana practice
  • Don't practice with neck, shoulder, or upper-back injury
  • Skip with knee or hip injury
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice
  • Avoid with vertigo or balance disorders

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Build Kukkutasana first; this is its reclined extension
  • Practice Garbha Pindasana (similar shape, easier entry) as a stepping stone
  • Hold for 3-5 breaths only at first

Traditional source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.24 — Named in HYP Ch. 1. The "stretched" or upward-facing tortoise — Kukkutasana reclined backward onto the back.
