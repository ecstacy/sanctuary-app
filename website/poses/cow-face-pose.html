Cow Face Pose

Gomukhasana·गोमुखासन·gomukhāsana

Also called: Cow-Faced Pose

Gomukhasana is two stretches in one — outer hips and shoulders. The asymmetry is informative: most users discover their dominant-side arm reaches behind the back further than the non-dominant. This pose makes the imbalance visible, which is the first step in correcting it.

Benefits

  • Releases the deep outer hip rotators and IT band — useful for runners and cyclists
  • Opens the shoulders in opposing directions (rotator-cuff balance)
  • Identifies asymmetry between the two sides — almost everyone has a 'tight' arm and a 'loose' arm
  • HYP positions it as one of the foundational seats — brief practice integrates well into a sitting practice

How to practise Cow Face Pose

  1. Sit on a folded blanket with legs extended.
  2. Bend the left knee. Place the left foot beside the right hip, knee pointing forward.
  3. Cross the right thigh over the left, stacking the knees. The right foot comes beside the left hip.
  4. Sit evenly on both sit bones — adjust the blanket if needed.
  5. Reach the right arm overhead. Bend the right elbow and let the right hand fall down your back.
  6. Bring the left arm behind your back from below. If accessible, hook the fingers together. Otherwise, hold a strap or towel between the hands.
  7. Lift the bottom elbow up. Lift the top elbow up. Switch BOTH arms and legs for the second side.

Suggested hold: 60s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Don't practice with shoulder injury, frozen shoulder, or rotator cuff tear
  • Skip with knee injury — the leg cross is intense
  • Pregnant women should skip the leg cross and modify with a simple cross-legged seat
  • Avoid with severe neck pain

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Use a strap or towel between the hands if they don't meet behind the back
  • Sit on a blanket if the knees don't stack comfortably
  • Practice the arms-only version with the legs in Sukhasana
  • Practice the legs-only version with the hands resting on the top knee

Traditional source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.20 — Named explicitly in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1. The legs in the pose are said to resemble a cow's face — knees stacked like the muzzle, feet flaring like the ears.
