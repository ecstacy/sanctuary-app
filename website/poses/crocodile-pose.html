Crocodile Pose

Makarasana·मकरासन·makarāsana

Also called: Crocodile

Crocodile is the recovery pose most users have never tried. The face-down geometry is unusual, but the diaphragmatic-breath training is unique — most other poses don't give the belly anything to press against. Use as a transition pose or as a 5-minute reset on a stressed day.

Benefits

  • Most accessible recovery pose in the catalog — works between deeper poses or as a complete practice
  • Trains diaphragmatic (belly) breathing through proprioceptive feedback from the floor
  • Decompresses the lower back through gentle gravitational stretch
  • Quietly therapeutic — used in some lineages for chronic lower-back rehab

How to practise Crocodile Pose

  1. Lie face down on a soft surface.
  2. Stack your forearms in front of you and rest your forehead on the forearms.
  3. Spread the legs slightly wider than hip-width apart, heels falling out to the sides.
  4. Let the body soften completely into the floor.
  5. Soften the jaw and the eyes.
  6. Breathe naturally into the belly. Each inhale presses the belly gently against the mat.
  7. Stay 3-5 minutes. Use this between deeper backbends or as a recovery pose.

Suggested hold: 180s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Pregnant women in mid-to-late pregnancy shouldn't lie face down
  • Skip with severe lower-back pain — try side-lying instead
  • Avoid with severe heartburn or hiatal hernia
  • Don't practice immediately after eating

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Place a thin folded blanket under the belly for support if the lower back complains
  • Use a bolster lengthwise under the chest for an upper-body lift variation
  • Practice with the head turned to one side instead of forehead-down — switch sides periodically
  • Substitute with Child's Pose if face-down isn't accessible

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern restorative pose. "Makara" = crocodile. Not enumerated in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika Ch. 1.
