Headstand

Sirsasana·शीर्षासन·śīrṣāsana

Also called: Salamba Sirsasana (supported), King of Asanas (Iyengar)

Sirsasana is the most consequential pose in the catalog — the benefits are real and the injury risk is real. Insist on teacher guidance for new practitioners. For app-only users, recommend Legs Up the Wall as an effective alternative until they're working with someone in person.

Benefits

  • Profound inversion — reverses venous flow, decompresses the spine
  • Builds extreme core, shoulder, and forearm strength
  • Concentration practice — most users learn what real focus feels like only after holding their first 60-second headstand
  • Iyengar called it the king of asanas paired with Sarvangasana the queen

How to practise Headstand

  1. WARNING: This is an advanced inversion. Practice with a teacher first. Improper form can cause cervical-spine injury.
  2. Kneel near a wall. Interlace the fingers and place the forearms on the mat, elbows shoulder-width apart.
  3. Place the crown of the head on the mat, the back of the head cradled in the interlaced hands.
  4. Lift the hips up like Down Dog. Walk the feet in toward the elbows.
  5. Bend the knees in toward the chest, then slowly extend the legs straight up toward the ceiling.
  6. Most of the weight should be in the forearms, NOT the head — only 10-20% on the crown.
  7. New practitioners: keep the hold short and come down well before fatigue. Come down slowly with the same control.

Suggested hold: 60s · Breath: Natural

Who should avoid or modify this pose

  • Do NOT practice without a teacher's guidance for at least the first month
  • Don't practice with neck injury, herniated disc, or any cervical-spine issue
  • Pregnant women shouldn't practice
  • Skip with high blood pressure, glaucoma, retinal detachment, or untreated heart conditions
  • Skip during menstruation per personal preference
  • Don't practice with severe asthma or hiatal hernia
  • Don't practice on a full stomach
  • If you cannot hold Down Dog for 60 seconds with steady form, you are not ready for Sirsasana

If you're pregnant, injured, or managing a condition, check with a qualified teacher or clinician before practising.

Modifications

  • Practice with a wall behind you for the first six months
  • Practice Tripod Headstand (head + two hands forming a triangle) — easier on the cervical spine for some bodies
  • Use a Headstand Bench / Sirsasana stool — no head pressure at all
  • Substitute Sarvangasana or Legs Up the Wall for similar nervous-system effect without head pressure

Traditional source: Modern hatha — Modern hatha. Some commentators link Sirsasana to verses on Viparita Karani mudra in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika; the discrete asana is a 20th-century codification.
